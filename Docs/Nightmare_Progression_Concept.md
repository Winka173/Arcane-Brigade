# Proposal: the shop falls asleep

## Approved finale clarification

The campaign ends at wave **20**, not wave 30. The final hall becomes a monumental toyshop theatre: cupboard ribs, watching locks, velvet and lanterns around an open fighting area. Architecture builds gradually before this hall, not through a black screen filter. Endless remains optional after the ending.

The Last Customer is distinct from the Outside Keeper. The hand, tongue and eye belong to the dreaming shop, not the man. Victory breaks the Customer's contract and mask; he survives holding an unsigned receipt. Hints that he sought a missing name remain ambiguous, not a newly asserted wife/child backstory or an excuse for treating people as inventory.

Two post-battle choices both reach a genuine free morning:

- **Close the shop:** accept goodbye without forgetting; the complete four-page dawn ends normally.
- **Leave one light on:** reach the same real dawn, carrying a key and a promise. A fifth illustrated afterword leaves one lamp on beneath the counter, with three quiet knocks. Returning is a possibility, never a debt.

This is a sequel hook, not an unfinished chapter, forced extra boss or false awakening. Optional rooms enrich the goodbye; they are not prerequisites. Rescued friends and resolved rooms preserve real comfort. Persistent promise flags are narrative only, not permanent combat stats. This clarification supersedes the original single-ending proposal below.

Approved concept. Runtime story progression and the eight-event narrative integration are documented in `Nightmare_Implementation.md`; that document also lists remaining authored cinematic work rather than claiming every proposed animation is finished.

The living shop is dreaming. The run descends through increasingly unstable layers of that dream. Familiar objects acquire unsettling anatomy and remember the player's decisions. A saved wave-based depth controls the main arc; elapsed play can add bounded atmospheric details without punishing slower players or worsening readability.

| Waves | Layer | Signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | The shop has not closed | Warm light, clear toy instrumentation, a few subtly delayed shadows. |
| 5–8 | After the last bell | Windows darken, objects shift between visits, the merchant watches an empty corner. |
| 9–12 | The dream leaks | Shelves stretch into darkness; receipts know the player's name; helpful objects reveal teeth and eyes. |
| 13–16 | The shop remembers | The hand, carpet tongue and keyhole eye reveal a common owner. Rooms retain traces of rescues, trades and letters. |
| 17–20 | The night refuses to end | Shelves resemble ribs, velvet breathes, windows become watchful eyes. Early musical themes return transformed. |

Keep heroes, hostile shots and attack warnings clear. Build dreamlike tension through architecture, art states, ambience, missing instruments, changed articulation and small narrative contradictions. Avoid blur, confusing control changes or mandatory jump scares. Friends the player rescued preserve warmth and recognition.

Ending proposal: **The Last Customer** arrives after the final wave, carrying receipts for the entire run and seeking to purchase every living object, including the protagonist. The final fight uses stamps, transactions and shop objects as readable attacks. Victory reveals the real door handle. The party pushes the door open together; dawn enters, the opening music returns in clear acoustic form, and rescued friends cross first. The bell rings once as the shop closes behind them. An object from the run rests on a real-world windowsill and moves faintly before the fade.

The primary ending must be reachable on the normal path. Secret events enrich who escapes, what is carried home and the final details; the ending must not require finding all eight rooms through random doors. Endless mode remains an explicit optional continuation after the ending.
